# Story 7.10: Reviewer backlog alerting

Status: done

<!-- Validation: create-story checklist applied 2026-06-02. -->

## Story

As a tenant administrator,
I want an alert when a reviewer's open-approval backlog crosses the threshold,
so that approval bottlenecks surface before they become risk.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Given a reviewer's open approval items, when the count of **open** items assigned to that reviewer for a `(tenant × reviewer)` pair **exceeds the threshold (> 25 open items, NFR46)**, then a backlog alert fires for that reviewer. "Open" = a `PendingApproval`-family queue item that is **not terminal/decided/resolved** (excludes Approved/Rejected/RevisionRequested/Cancelled/Executed/Failed/Skipped/`resolved`, mirroring the Story 7.8 `ApprovalPriorityScorer.IsPending` and the Story 7.7 `EscalationPolicyEvaluator.IsTerminalOrResolved` exclusions). The count is **measured server-side**, keyed strictly by `(tenant-ref-from-authenticated-binding × reviewer-ref)`, and the decision is **deterministic and pure** given the injected `ISystemClock` and the tenant-bound queue snapshot. Boundary is decided and tested: exactly **25 open items does NOT alert**; the **26th** open item crosses (strictly-greater-than, mirroring the escalation age-threshold `>` semantics). [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR46`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.10`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/EscalationPolicyEvaluator.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Projections/ApprovalPriorityScorer.cs`]

2. Given a backlog alert fires, when it is produced, then it conveys exactly three reviewer-attention signals: the reviewer's **safe identity ref**, the **backlog depth** (count of open items), and the **oldest open item's age** — server-measured in UTC via `ISystemClock` (`now − freshnessTimestampUtc`, falling back to the projector's server-measured `AgeSeconds`, clamped ≥ 0), **never** client/item-supplied time and never a per-process wall clock. The oldest-item age computation reuses the `EscalationPolicyEvaluator.EffectiveAgeSeconds` discipline exactly. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR46`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.10`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/EscalationPolicyEvaluator.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Audit/ISystemClock.cs`]

3. Given the alert is an **aggregate** `(tenant × reviewer)` signal, when it is built and delivered, then it carries **no item-specific context** and exposes **no restricted detail** — no project name, evidence, mailbox content/subject, file metadata, audit reason detail, recipient PII beyond safe refs, provider payload, prompt, command body, claim, header, token, or secret (NFR2). Because it is aggregate, the underlying `NotificationStateEvent` carries a **null `ItemProjectRef`**, so `NotificationRoutingResolver` resolves it to `NotificationContentVisibility.MetadataRedacted` and the delivery's `ItemRef` is dropped — indistinguishable from safe-not-found. The reviewer identity is a **metadata-safe ref** (the same safe-token class already emitted as `queue-assignee:`/`queue-reviewer:` in audit), not restricted content; it is the subject of the alert, never a leak of an item the recipient lacks authority over. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR46`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR2`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/NotificationRoutingResolver.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/NotificationContentVisibility.cs`]

4. Given aggregation is performed, when the backlog count and oldest-item age are computed, then they are keyed strictly by `(tenant-ref-from-authenticated-binding × reviewer-ref)` — the tenant comes from the authenticated gateway binding (carried on `NotificationStateEvent.TenantRef`/the queue snapshot), **never** from item refs, queue config, reviewer-supplied data, or correlation ids — and one tenant's or one reviewer's backlog can never trigger, suppress, inflate, or leak into another's alert. Items with **no `AssigneeRef`** are not attributed to any reviewer (they cannot create a phantom backlog for a real reviewer); this exclusion is stated in completion notes. A redacted-from-`B` item is never counted in `A`'s backlog. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/architecture.md#API & Communication Patterns`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR2`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/NotificationRoutingResolver.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs`]

5. Given a reviewer backlog crosses the threshold, when the alert is routed, then the recipient is the **tenant administrator** (`AdminRole.TenantAdmin`), resolved through the **existing** `NotificationRoutingResolver` audience + scope path (`AdminAuthorityEvaluator.HasHumanRole`/`HasHumanAdminScope`) — **not a new bespoke authority check**. The reviewer is **not** the recipient of their own backlog alert; the tenant admin is alerted so the bottleneck surfaces to someone who can rebalance it. Only candidates holding the configured tenant-admin role and scope receive the alert. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.10`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/NotificationRoutingResolver.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Governance/Admin` (AdminAuthorityEvaluator); `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Enums/AdminRole.cs`]

6. Given the alert threshold is configurable, when a tenant administrator tunes it, then the threshold is adjustable only through a **closed, schema-bounded** Tenant Policy knob whose value is bounded **at or below the NFR46 governance maximum (≤ 25)** — a tenant may **lower** the threshold (to be alerted *sooner*) but can **never raise it above 25** (which would suppress alerts and hide a bottleneck), introduce a new dimension, or supply a free-form expression. Out-of-range (including above-maximum), wrong-type, NaN/Infinity, or undeclared values are rejected with the existing safe reason codes (`range_invalid:`/`wrong_value_type:`/unknown-knob) and the evaluator falls back to the declared safe default (= 25, the NFR46 maximum). This is a **Standard** triage-tuning knob — not security-sensitive — so **no** blanket two-person rule applies. If implemented as a fixed governance constant instead of a knob, the constant equals 25 and this is stated in completion notes. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR46`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75d`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Commands/NotificationThrottleCeilings.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Commands/TenantPolicyContracts.cs`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-9-notification-throttling-and-digest-rollup.md#Completion Notes List`]

7. Given a backlog alert is fired and delivered, when the pre-commit audit for that alert is unavailable, then the alert **fails closed** — no durable/observable delivery side effect occurs (mirroring the Story 7.7 escalation `AuditUnavailable` outcome). When audit succeeds, the envelope carries **metadata-only refs**: tenant ref, reviewer ref, `backlog-depth:<n>`, `backlog-oldest-age-seconds:<n>`, `backlog-threshold:<n>`, `notification-state-class:<token>`, `notification-channel:<token>`, `admin-scope:<token>`, `recipient-role:<token>`, reason code, correlation id, UTC timestamp — **never** project content, evidence, file metadata, audit reason detail, provider payloads, prompts, command bodies, recipient PII beyond safe refs, claims, headers, tokens, or secrets. Exactly **one** metadata-only envelope is written per fired alert. This reuses the established per-event fail-closed audit pattern from escalation (Story 7.7) and notification delivery (Story 7.9). [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR15a`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75g`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Audit/AuditEnvelopeFactory.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/EscalationEvaluationCoordinator.cs`]

## Dev Notes

### Scope Boundaries

- Story 7.10 implements exactly **one** NFR46 mechanism: the **reviewer backlog alert at > 25 open items**. It is the **fourth** NFR46 anti-fatigue mechanism, layered on the notification delivery seam shipped by Story 7.6, the clock-injected evaluator/coordinator + fail-closed-audit pattern shipped by Story 7.7, the approval-queue pending/`AssigneeRef` semantics shipped by Story 7.8, and the closed-bounded-knob precedent shipped by Stories 7.8/7.9.
- It builds directly on **Story 7.6** (the metadata-only `INotificationSink`/`NotificationDelivery`/`NotificationStateEvent`/`NotificationContentVisibility` seam and `NotificationRoutingResolver` audience + redaction path), **Story 7.7** (the pure, clock-injected `EscalationPolicyEvaluator` + `EscalationEvaluationCoordinator` evaluate → per-event fail-closed audit → deliver → count pattern, `EscalationDelivery` wrapper, `EffectiveAgeSeconds` server-measured age), **Story 7.8** (`AdminQueueSummaryProjectionItem`, `ApprovalPriorityScorer.IsPending`/terminal statuses, `AssigneeRef` reviewer attribution), and **Story 7.9** (the closed-bounded threshold-knob precedent and its validator-guard pattern). **Reuse those; do not fork the delivery seam, the authority/redaction path, the age discipline, or the audit pattern.**
- The alert is an **aggregate per-reviewer signal** delivered to the **tenant admin**, not an item-level notification and not a new approval authority. It must never become a covert channel for project content, evidence, file metadata, mailbox content, audit reasons, recipient PII, or provider/AI payloads (NFR2). It carries only the reviewer's safe ref, backlog depth, and oldest-item age.
- It does NOT implement the other NFR46 mechanisms: prioritization + grouping = Story 7.8 (done); throttle/digest = Story 7.9 (done); **the rubber-stamp-rate observable (> 15% rolling-7-day, < 5 s approvals) and median/p95 time-in-queue dashboards** = Story 7.11 / Epic 8. Do not implement the rubber-stamp observable here — but keep the aggregation shaped so 7.11/Epic 8 can layer on it.
- The **runtime alert caller / periodic binding is deferred** (consistent with Story 7.6's deferred FR72 delivery caller, 7.7's deferred escalation runtime, and 7.9's deferred throttle runtime). This story provides the deterministic aggregator/evaluator, the alert record, the coordinator wiring, and the audit envelope — not an always-on `BackgroundService`.
