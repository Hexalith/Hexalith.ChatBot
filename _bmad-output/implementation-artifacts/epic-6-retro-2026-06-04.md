## What Went Well

- Server-owned authority held. UI, CLI, MCP, workers, and provider adapters did not get their own sender-authority logic.
- `draft-only` stayed local to ChatBot and did not accidentally become a Microsoft Graph draft or send path.
- Outbound send required approval, current authority recomputation, idempotency admission, audit readiness, and aggregate acceptance before any adapter side effect.
- Inbound authenticity metadata stayed non-blocking at intake while still becoming available to association, risk, audit, and reviewer projections.
- External-sender and authenticity strictness became routing guards over deterministic scoring rather than hidden score changes.
- OpenAPI/client regeneration and generated-client fixture updates were handled repeatedly as contract surfaces changed.
- Story reviews produced meaningful fixes and added regression coverage rather than only updating prose.

## Challenges

- Several safety defects only surfaced in senior review:
  - Story 6.1 initially masked missing outbound service-client grant as `approval-missing`.
  - Story 6.2 initially trusted submitted source actor identity too far.
  - Story 6.3 initially under-bound outbound send execution to the paired approval request.
  - Story 6.4 initially dropped later ordered `Authentication-Results` verdicts.
  - Story 6.5 initially applied strictness routing to external-sender posture but not inbound authenticity anomalies.
- Metadata-only output was difficult to keep consistent across audit refs, problem details, projections, worker results, and generated contract shapes.
- The Epic 6 aggregate status in sprint tracking lagged story reality.
- Documentation did not receive an Epic 6-specific update audit before this retrospective.
- The future provider-adapter path remains sensitive: Graph/Exchange behavior is evidence and transport only; ChatBot policy, approval, and audit remain authoritative.

## Key Insights

1. Sender authority is a server decision, not a surface argument or provider permission mirror.
2. Approval records must bind the exact approved scope: command name, allowlist version, policy snapshot, source refs, sender authority, recipients, and context refs.
3. Evidence freshness is enforcement. Stale or expired send evidence cannot be treated as a display-only warning.
4. Authenticity signals should not block mailbox intake by themselves; they become safe routing and review metadata downstream.
5. External-sender posture and authenticity anomalies need the same strictness policy path, or the policy surface becomes misleading.
6. Review checklists for this codebase should explicitly include actor binding, authority recomputation, metadata bounds, OpenAPI drift, generated-client drift, and story File List reality.
