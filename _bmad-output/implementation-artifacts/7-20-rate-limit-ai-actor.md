# Story 7.20: Rate-limit AI actor

Status: done

<!-- Validation: create-story checklist applied 2026-06-02. -->

## Story

As an authorized **policy administrator**,
I want to rate-limit an AI actor through a single-actor, schema-bounded policy mutation,
so that proposal volume does not overwhelm reviewers or queues — **command admission** applies the bounded per-window limit and returns a typed, redacted rate-limit response when the AI actor exceeds it (surfacing approval-fatigue and backlog effects), while unrelated AI actors, service clients, and tenants keep their own independent budgets and are protected from degradation where isolation is possible.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Given an AI actor exceeding proposal or execution thresholds, when an authorized **human policy administrator** configures a rate limit, then a durable per-AI-actor rate-limit budget is recorded and **command admission applies the bounded limit** to that AI actor's proposals/commands. This is a **standard, single-actor policy mutation** — explicitly **NOT** the Story 7.18/7.19 FR75d two-person control: FR74's decomposition guidance states "disable and quarantine are security-sensitive admin operations (per FR75d two-person rule); **rate-limit is a standard policy mutation**." So the command follows the **single-actor** authorization shape of the Story 7.17 `SubmitServiceClientRateLimit` / Story 7.14 `SubmitMailboxSourceRateLimit` (one authority check, no approver), **not** the submit→second-person-approve flow of `SubmitAiActorDisable`/`SubmitAiActorQuarantine`; there is **no** `Approve…` counterpart, **no** distinct-approver guard, **no** `AcceptedCommandDispatcher` branch, and **no** `AiActorControlState`/lifecycle transition (rate-limit is a *parameter*, not a control state). [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.20`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR74`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-17-rate-limit-service-client.md`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Commands/ServiceClientControlContracts.cs`]

2. Given the rate-limit value, when it is validated, then it is **bounded by the Tenant Policy Schema** (FR75): the configured per-window proposal budget is accepted only within a declared, closed, non-negative min/max range, and an out-of-range / wrong-type / undeclared value is **rejected with a safe reason code** (and, at the enforcement seam, falls back to safe defaults — never silently raising the cap). Mirror the closed `ServiceClientRateLimitBounds` discipline from Story 7.17 (`Minimum`/`Maximum`/`SafeDefaults`/`IsWithinBounds`, itself mirroring Story 7.14 `MailboxRateLimitBounds` / Story 7.9 `NotificationThrottleCeilings`) — a tenant cannot introduce a new window dimension or a custom formula. The bound is a Standard (non-security-sensitive) knob, so no two-person rule applies. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Commands/ServiceClientRateLimitBounds.cs:14`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Commands/MailboxRateLimitBounds.cs:14`]

3. Given authorization runs, when the command is evaluated, then it is permitted only for a **human policy administrator** — gate on **`AdminAuthorityEvaluator.HasHumanAdminScope(actor.Principal, AdminScope.Policy)`** (AI-actor governance is the policy-admin's domain per Story 7.2 "Policy-admin scope … and AI action policy"; the epic persona is explicitly "policy administrator"). This is the **same divergence Stories 7.18/7.19 applied** and the **key difference from the 7.17 service-client rate-limit**, which gated on `HasHumanTenantAdmin` because no finer service-client scope exists — here a finer scope (`AdminScope.Policy`) **does** exist and is the natural home. A `tenant-admin` still qualifies because `AdminScopes.ScopesForRole(TenantAdmin)` returns the full scope set (FR75a union) — so this is **not** a relaxation. The operation is **denied for service clients and AI actors** even when they carry admin-looking claims (the human-actor gate inside `HasHumanAdminScope`/`IsHumanActor`), and denied for non-policy human scopes (`mailbox-admin`/`compliance-admin`/`operations-admin`-only). There is **no** second-person/distinct-approver requirement; a single authorized human policy-admin applies it. Denial uses `ChatBotAuthorizationReasonCodes.AuthorizationDenied`. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.20`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.2`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75a`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Governance/Admin/AdminAuthorityEvaluator.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs:135`]

4. Given the rate limit is applied (committed), when it is recorded, then the operation records — with **no skip-audit path** (FR74, FR75g, NFR15a) — the **actor** (requester identity / actor type), **scope used** (`admin-scope:policy`), **subject** (the safe AI-actor ref / its `ServiceClientId`), **reason** (documented justification reason code), **old state** (the prior budget / `unlimited`), **new state** (the new bounded budget), **window token**, **policy snapshot id**, and **timestamp**, via the metadata-only `AuditEnvelope`. There is **no** `StateTransition` ref (rate-limit is not a lifecycle transition — old/new "state" are budgets). The audit is written through the existing fail-closed pre-commit seam (`IAuditWriter.RecordPreCommitAsync` via `CommandGateway`): when the pre-commit audit is unavailable, **no durable rate-limit state is written and no enforcement side effect occurs** (fail closed), reusing the disable/`CommandGateway` audit-unavailable behavior. No admin role bypasses NFR15a/NFR50a. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.20`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75g`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Audit/AuditEnvelopeFactory.cs:1753`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/CommandGateway.cs`]

5. Given an AI actor has a rate-limit budget configured, when that AI actor submits a command — including an `ExecuteLowRiskAIAssistance` / `ExecuteApprovedAIAction` AI proposal — and its recent **admitted-proposal** count in the trailing rolling window has reached the effective budget, then **command admission enforces the limit at `ServiceClientGrantValidator`** — returning `ChatBotAuthorizationResult.Denied(ChatBotAuthorizationReasonCodes.AiActorRateLimited)` (a typed, redacted rate-limit response) so the over-budget proposal is **not dispatched, never reaches the downstream `AiActionApprovalGate`, and no restricted data is returned**. This check is the AI-actor branch of the **final admission gate** — it sits **after** the AI-actor disable/quarantine control-state block, **after** the service-client control-state block, **and after** the grant scope/allowlist checks — so only otherwise-fully-admissible proposals count against the budget and a disabled/quarantined/expired/revoked/over-/under-scoped command keeps its own precise reason code (rate-limit never masks a security denial). When the AI actor is **under** budget the proposal is admitted normally. The trailing-window count is computed with the established clock-injected window math (`NotificationThrottleEvaluator.CountInTrailingWindow`: server-measured UTC age, never client/item-supplied time), keyed per-`(tenant × AI-actor)`. `ServiceClientGrantValidator` already runs only for `service`/`ai` actor types and already has `ISystemClock` injected. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.20`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ServiceClientGrantValidator.cs:141`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Notifications/NotificationThrottleEvaluator.cs:63`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/AiActionApprovalGate.cs`]

6. Given rate-limiting is in effect, when queue/capacity impact is observed, then admission **exposes capacity-impact metrics that surface approval-fatigue and backlog effects** — at minimum the effective budget, the observed trailing-window admitted-proposal count, and whether this proposal was denied (throttled) for the AI actor — carried as safe, finite integer tokens at the audit/observable seam (mirror the Story 7.17 `ServiceClientRateLimitObservation` / Story 7.14 `MailboxRateLimitObservation` shape: integer/rational arithmetic, never floats). Rate-limiting **protects unrelated tenants/AI-actors from degradation where isolation is possible** (NFR30, NFR18): each AI actor's budget and trailing-window counter are **independent**, so throttling a noisy AI actor never throttles or starves a sibling AI actor, a service client, or another tenant. Full operational-dashboard wiring is Epic 8 and is out of scope here — this story exposes the metric at the audit/observable seam only. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.20`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR30`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/IServiceClientRateLimitProvider.cs:35`]

7. Given a command is denied for a rate-limited AI actor, when guidance is surfaced, then **safe recovery guidance** is shown from the **finite message catalog** — a safe headline (≤ 80 chars), a one-line reason conveying "proposal capacity temporarily limited to protect reviewers/queues; retry shortly", a next-action of **`retry-later`** (transient, **not** `request-access` — the AI actor is not disabled or quarantined), and a **transient** `dependency-degraded` reason (**not** `disabled-action`) — using `ChatBotMessageCatalog` / `ChatBotMessageCodes` / `ChatBotMessageNextActions` / `ChatBotDisabledActionReasons`, never raw error text. This matches the Story 7.17 service-client-rate-limit / Story 7.14 mailbox-rate-limit catalog choice and is deliberately distinct from the terminal `ai_actor_disabled`/`ai_actor_quarantined` entries (`request-access` + `disabled-action`). The guidance never names an unauthorized project/file/party/mailbox-content/audit detail (NFR2); EN/FR via existing localization; stable machine tokens (reason codes, state tokens, correlation ids) stay untranslated. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR76`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR2`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Messages/ChatBotMessageCatalog.cs:462`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Messages/ChatBotMessageNextActions.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Messages/ChatBotDisabledActionReasons.cs`]

8. Given the rate-limit subject and audit refs are metadata, when the command and audit envelope are serialized, then they carry **only safe, finite tokens** — the safe AI-actor ref (its `ServiceClientId`), `admin-operation:ai-actor-rate-limit` / `admin-scope:policy`, the reason code, old/new budget values, the window token, policy-snapshot id, correlation id, UTC timestamp — and **never** service-client/AI credentials, OAuth grant fingerprints, bearer tokens, secrets, model prompts/completions, raw claims/headers, delegated-user PII, mailbox/project/proposal/evidence content, or raw GUIDs that are not already safe identifiers (NFR2, NFR4, NFR5). The subject is identified by its existing safe id (`ServiceClientGrant.ServiceClientId` via `AuditMetadata.SafeOptionalToken` / `IsSafeStableIdentifier`). Tenant scope comes **only** from the authenticated gateway binding — never from the command body. ## Dev Notes

### Scope Boundaries

- Story 7.20 is the **ninth** cell of the FR74 enforcement series (7.12–7.26: five subject classes — mailbox / service-client / **AI-actor** / command-capability / outbound — × three actions — disable / quarantine / rate-limit). It implements exactly **one** cell: **AI actor × rate-limit**. Disable (7.18) and quarantine (7.19) are **security-sensitive** admin operations gated by the **FR75d two-person rule**; **rate-limit (this story) is a standard, single-actor policy mutation** bounded by the Tenant Policy Schema. **Do not** implement any other subject class, the disable/quarantine actions, or a future rate-limit-release/reset flow here. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR74`]
- **This story is a cross-product: take the 7.17 single-actor rate-limit pattern and apply it to the 7.18/7.19 AI-actor subject + admission seam.** Read 7.17 (service-client rate-limit, commit `8c5c46e`) for the *single-actor command / bounds / window / direct-activation aggregate / audit-without-StateTransition / transient catalog / no-dispatcher-branch / no-lifecycle-transition / final-gate-at-`ServiceClientGrantValidator`* shape, and 7.18/7.19 (AI-actor disable/quarantine, commits `cfb16d3`/`2579a42`) for the *AI-actor subject + `HasHumanAdminScope(AdminScope.Policy)` authority + AI-actor-gated admission branch + dedicated AI-actor control plane + read-side deferral*. The result is the AI-actor cell that is both single-actor **and** enforced at command admission.
- **The two meaningful divergences from the 7.17 service-client-rate-limit template — read these first:**
  1. **Subject + authority = policy-admin, not tenant-admin** (the 7.18/7.19 divergence). Subject is an **AI actor** (`AiActorRef`/`ServiceClientId`), and authorization is **`HasHumanAdminScope(AdminScope.Policy)`** (AI-actor governance is the AI-action policy domain per Story 7.2). 7.17 used `HasHumanTenantAdmin` because no finer service-client scope exists; for the AI actor a finer scope **does** exist. A `tenant-admin` still passes via the FR75a scope union. **Rejected:** `HasHumanTenantAdmin` (too coarse; ignores the named policy-admin persona) and a new `AdminScope.AiActor` (scope-creep).
  2. **Distinct AI-actor budget + reason at the shared final gate.** The `ServiceClientGrantValidator` rate-limit final gate already runs for `service` **and** `ai` actors (from 7.17), but it must give an AI actor a **distinct** `ai_actor_rate_limited` reason from its **own** budget provider/history — never the service-client `service_client_rate_limited` path. Branch the final gate on actor type, exactly as the disable/quarantine checks separate the AI-actor control plane from the service-client plane. There is **no** intake-routing branch and **no** worker change — admission for an AI actor is enforced entirely in the Authorization stage.
- **Same as 7.17 (do not reintroduce the two-person machinery):** single-actor — **no** `Approve…` command, **no** pending-approval event/dictionary, **no** `AcceptedCommandDispatcher` branch/distinct-approver guard, **no** aggregate distinct-approver re-check. **Not** a control state / lifecycle transition — **no** `AiActorControlState`/`LifecycleStates`/`LifecycleTransitionValidator`/`CommandSubmissionLifecycleTransitionGuard` change; "old state"/"new state" in the audit = old budget / new budget. **Transient** catalog guidance (`retry-later` + `dependency-degraded`), not the terminal `request-access` + `disabled-action`.
- **Bounded by the Tenant Policy Schema (FR75).** The budget value is accepted only within a declared closed range. Reuse the `ServiceClientRateLimitBounds` discipline: `Minimum`/`Maximum`/`SafeDefaults`/`IsWithinBounds`, out-of-bounds → safe default (= maximum), never raise the cap. A tenant cannot add a new window dimension or a custom formula.
- **Isolation (NFR30/NFR18).** Each AI actor has an independent budget + trailing-window counter. Throttling a noisy AI actor must never throttle a sibling AI actor, a service client, or another tenant. Tenant identity is always from the **authenticated gateway binding**, never the command body. The subject and all audit refs are **metadata-only, safe tokens** (NFR2).
- Rate-limit affects **future command admission only** (including future AI proposals). It must never delete, rewrite, or hide already-committed proposals, commands, audit envelopes, associations, files, approvals, conversation content, or their audit trails (NFR17, FR75c).

### "AI proposals" — what gets throttled and where (unchanged framing from 7.18/7.19)

- AI proposals are submitted as **commands** by the AI actor: `ExecuteLowRiskAIAssistance` (and `ExecuteApprovedAIAction`). They run through the shared command pipeline (FR81a): **Authorization stage** (`ParticipantAuthorizationStage` → `ServiceClientGrantValidator`) → **Approval gate** (`AiActionApprovalGate` → `DefaultAiActionPolicyEvaluator`). Authorization runs first, so throttling a rate-limited AI actor at `ServiceClientGrantValidator` is **fail-closed before** the proposal reaches `AiActionApprovalGate` or any policy evaluation. **No** change to `AiActionApprovalGate`/`DefaultAiActionPolicyEvaluator` is needed (and none should be made). The over-budget proposal never reaches a reviewer — that is precisely the "approval-fatigue and backlog" protection the epic AC calls for (the throttled count is the backlog-reduction signal).
