# Story 7.18: Disable AI actor

Status: done

<!-- Validation: create-story checklist applied 2026-06-02. -->

## Story

As an authorized **policy administrator**,
I want to disable a misbehaving AI actor under a two-person rule,
so that unsafe AI mediation cannot continue — **future AI proposals (and any other command) from that AI actor fail closed at the command-admission pipeline** — while every prior proposal and audit record stays intact and reconstructable, and safe recovery guidance is shown.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Given an AI actor producing unsafe or policy-violating proposals, when an authorized **human policy admin** proposes a **disable** and a **second** authorized human policy admin approves it (FR75d two-person rule), then the AI actor transitions to a durable **Disabled** governance control state and **future AI proposals and commands from that AI actor fail closed** at the command-admission pipeline, while **unrelated AI actors and other actors continue normally** (isolation, NFR30/NFR58). The disable is a security-sensitive FR74 governance control — **not** the Epic 5 Keycloak-sourced grant lifecycle (`ServiceClientGrant.IsRevoked`/`ExpiresAt`) — so it MUST go through the same submit→second-person-approve flow established for the Story 7.15 service-client disable (`SubmitServiceClientDisable`→`ApproveServiceClientDisable`) and Story 7.12 mailbox-source disable; a single-actor disable never takes effect. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.18`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR74`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75d`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-15-disable-service-client.md`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Operations/GovernedOperationAggregate.cs`]

2. Given the disable proposal and approval, when authorization runs, then the operation is permitted only for a **human policy administrator** — gate on **`AdminAuthorityEvaluator.HasHumanAdminScope(principal, AdminScope.Policy)`** (AI-action governance is the policy-admin's domain per Story 7.2 "Policy-admin scope … and AI action policy"; the epic persona is explicitly "policy administrator"). This is the **key divergence from Stories 7.15/7.16/7.17**, which gated service-client governance on `HasHumanTenantAdmin` because no finer service-client scope exists — here a finer scope (`AdminScope.Policy`) **does** exist and is the natural home. A `tenant-admin` still qualifies because `AdminScopes.ScopesForRole(TenantAdmin)` returns the full scope set (FR75a union) — so this is **not** a relaxation. The operation is **denied for service clients and AI actors** even when they carry admin-looking claims (the human-actor gate inside `HasHumanAdminScope`/`IsHumanActor`), and denied for non-policy human scopes (`mailbox-admin`/`compliance-admin`/`operations-admin`-only). The approving admin MUST be a **different person** from the proposer: both `RequesterRef != ApproverRef` (Ordinal safe-token compare) and `RequesterActorId != approver envelope UserId` are enforced — at the gateway validation stage, the accepted-command dispatcher, **and** re-checked in the aggregate, mirroring `IsValidServiceClientDisableApproval`, `AcceptedCommandDispatcher`'s distinct-approver guard, and `GovernedOperationAggregate.Handle(ApproveServiceClientDisable)`. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.18`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.2`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75a`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75d`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Governance/Admin/AdminAuthorityEvaluator.cs:10`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs:78`]

3. Given the disable is applied (approval committed), when it is recorded, then the operation records — with **no skip-audit path** (FR74, FR75g, NFR15a) — the **actor** (admin identity / actor type), **scope used** (`admin-scope:policy`), **subject** (the safe AI-actor ref / its `ServiceClientId`), **reason** (documented justification reason code), **old state** (`Active`), **new state** (`Disabled`), **policy snapshot id**, and **timestamp**, via a metadata-only `AuditEnvelope` whose `StateTransition` is `"Active->Disabled"`. The audit is written through the existing fail-closed pre-commit seam (`IAuditWriter.RecordPreCommitAsync` via `CommandGateway`): when the pre-commit audit is unavailable, **no durable disable state is written and no admission-blocking side effect occurs** (fail closed), reusing the disable/`CommandGateway` audit-unavailable behavior. No admin role bypasses NFR15a/NFR50a. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.18`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75g`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR15a`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Audit/AuditEnvelopeFactory.cs`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/CommandGateway.cs`]

4. Given an AI actor is Disabled, when that AI actor submits **any command** — including an `ExecuteLowRiskAIAssistance` / `ExecuteApprovedAIAction` AI proposal — then the authorization stage **fails closed before admission**: `ServiceClientGrantValidator` (which already runs for `service` **and** `ai` actor types) returns `ChatBotAuthorizationResult.Denied("ai_actor_disabled")` for the `ai` actor whose `ServiceClientId` is in the disabled-AI-actor set, **before** the grant scope/allowlist checks and before the proposal ever reaches the downstream `AiActionApprovalGate` — so no AI proposal from the disabled actor is admitted and no restricted data is returned. A proposal/command from any **other (still-Active)** AI actor for the same tenant is unaffected (isolation). The new `ai_actor_disabled` reason is **distinct from** `service_client_disabled` (the 7.15 service-client governance control) and from the Epic 5 `service_client_grant_revoked` (Keycloak grant revocation) — it reflects the FR74 AI-actor governance control. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.18`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ServiceClientGrantValidator.cs:82`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/ChatBotAuthorizationReasonCodes.cs:33`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/AiActionApprovalGate.cs`]

5. Given an AI actor is disabled, when its prior activity is inspected, then **all previously recorded proposals, commands, audit envelopes, and workflow items that originated from that AI actor remain visible, intact, and fully reconstructable** — disable affects **only future admission**, never already-committed records, proposals, associations, files, approvals, conversation content, or their audit trails (NFR17 visible/recoverable states; FR75c admins cannot mutate project-level records). Disable is reversible by a future re-enable flow; this story does not delete or rewrite any prior record. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR17`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75c`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-15-disable-service-client.md`]

6. Given an actor encounters a disabled AI actor (admin viewing status, or an admission blocked), when guidance is surfaced, then **safe recovery guidance** is shown from the **finite message catalog** — a safe headline (≤ 80 chars), a one-line reason from a finite set, a next-action pointing to the responsible role (re-enable is a policy-admin/two-person action), and a `disabled-action` reason — using `ChatBotMessageCatalog` / `ChatBotMessageCodes` / `ChatBotDisabledActionReasons`, never raw error text. No surface names an unauthorized project/file/party/mailbox-content/audit detail (NFR2); EN/FR via existing localization; stable machine tokens (reason codes, state tokens, correlation ids) stay untranslated. ## Dev Notes

### Scope Boundaries

- Story 7.18 is the **seventh** cell of the FR74 enforcement series (7.12–7.26: five subject classes — mailbox / service-client / **AI-actor** / command-capability / outbound — × three actions — disable / quarantine / rate-limit). It implements exactly **one** cell: **AI actor × disable**. Disable (this story) and quarantine (7.19) are **security-sensitive** admin operations gated by the **FR75d two-person rule**; rate-limit (7.20) is a standard policy mutation. **Do not** implement quarantine, rate-limit, any other subject class, or a future re-enable/release flow here. - **This story is a near-verbatim mirror of Story 7.15 (disable service client), retargeted from the service-client subject to the AI-actor subject, with two meaningful divergences (read these first):**
  1. **Authorization scope = policy-admin, not tenant-admin.** The epic persona is "policy administrator", and AI-action governance is the policy-admin's domain (Story 7.2 ships the AI action policy under `AdminScope.Policy`). Gate on **`HasHumanAdminScope(actor.Principal, AdminScope.Policy)`** (as the existing policy-admin branches at `ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs:78/84/91/181/188` do). 7.15/7.16/7.17 used `HasHumanTenantAdmin` only because **no** finer service-client scope exists; for the AI actor a finer scope **does** exist. A `tenant-admin` still passes because `AdminScopes.ScopesForRole(TenantAdmin)` returns the full scope set (FR75a union) — so this is **not** a relaxation. **Rejected alternatives:** `HasHumanTenantAdmin` (too coarse; ignores the named policy-admin persona) and a brand-new `AdminScope.AiActor` (scope-creep; `Policy` already governs AI mediation).
  2. **The subject (AI actor) shares the `ServiceClientId` identifier space and the `ServiceClientGrantValidator` seam with service clients.** An AI actor is just an authenticated identity whose `actor_type` claim resolves to `"ai"` (vs `"service"`) — see `ClaimsAuthenticationStage.ResolveActorType` (`ClaimsAuthenticationStage.cs:34`); both carry a `ServiceClientId`/`ServiceClientGrant` and both flow through `ServiceClientGrantValidator` (`RequiresGrant` returns true for `service` **and** `ai`). The AI-actor disable therefore enforces at the **same validator**, but as a **new branch gated on `actor.ActorType == "ai"`** with a **dedicated AI-actor control plane** (its own `AiActorControlState`, `_disabledAiActors` set, `ai_actor_disabled` reason, `admin-operation:ai-actor-disable` audit) so the audited subject/reason are the AI-actor class, not the service-client class. **Rejected alternative:** reuse the service-client disabled set / `service_client_disabled` reason — that would conflate two distinct FR74 subject classes and misreport the audit subject.
- **Same as 7.15 (preserve the two-person machinery):** submit→second-person-approve; a single-actor disable only ever produces a pending approval. Distinct approver enforced **three times** (gateway validator, accepted-command dispatcher, aggregate re-check), `RequesterRef != ApproverRef` Ordinal AND `RequesterActorId != envelope.UserId`. Control state via the fixed FR74 path, never the Epic 5 grant-lifecycle (`IsRevoked`). Terminal catalog guidance (`request-access` + `disabled-action`), not transient.
- The disable affects **future admission only** (including future AI proposals). It must never delete, rewrite, or hide already-committed proposals, commands, audit envelopes, associations, files, approvals, or conversation content (NFR17, FR75c). It is reversible by a future re-enable flow (out of scope here).
- Tenant identity is always from the **authenticated gateway binding**, never the command body. The subject (AI actor) and all audit refs are **metadata-only, safe tokens** (NFR2); credentials, OAuth grant fingerprints, model prompts/completions, and delegated-user PII never appear (NFR4/NFR5).
