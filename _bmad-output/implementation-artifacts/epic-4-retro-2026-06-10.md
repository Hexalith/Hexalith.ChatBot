## What Went Well

- Epic 4 stayed inside the established contract/client/server/gateway/projection/UI architecture instead of creating a separate AI path.
- Trust boundaries improved with review pressure: unknown commands cannot spoof low-risk metadata, unknown action classes fail closed, and approved execution uses a distinct AI command allowlist.
- Low-risk execution remained conservative: policy-false, unavailable policy, missing authorization, missing package, and stale corrected context do not call the provider.
- Approval and refusal states are durable, localized, metadata-only, and projected back into the project conversation.
- Correction invalidation became structural: stale AI proposals are invalidated across proposal, approval, execution, projection, and UI layers.
- The June 10 re-reviews added or confirmed endpoint-level projection coverage for low-risk outcomes, approved execution, refusal, and correction invalidation.

## Challenges

- Projection wiring remained the recurring weak spot. Multiple stories initially proved translators directly before review required real subscriber/endpoint coverage.
- File-list and validation-artifact reconciliation lagged behind implementation changes in several stories.
- Story 4.2 retained a medium integration-completeness finding: the review panel was not mounted on the S1 page and the convert/disposition write path was absent from `ProjectConversationService`.
- Some low-severity provenance and fidelity follow-ups remain recorded in story reviews, including low-risk-route evidence freshness and omitted policy snapshot fallback in approved execution.
- The reliable validation path in this sandbox remains solution build plus compiled xUnit v3 runners; VSTest socket behavior is still unsuitable here.

## Key Insights

1. Governed AI mediation is command admission, not a chat feature.
2. Server-owned metadata is the trust boundary; adapters submit typed contracts and cannot classify, approve, audit, or allowlist locally.
3. Low-risk execution still needs the full safety floor: authorization, current context, policy, idempotency, audit, projection, and refusal behavior.
4. Projection endpoint wiring is a release-critical behavior, not a secondary plumbing detail.
5. Safe UI states are security states. `not-yet-produced`, unavailable, redacted, blocked, failed, and allowed must remain distinct.
6. Correction propagation must invalidate downstream AI work by construction, not by warning text alone.
