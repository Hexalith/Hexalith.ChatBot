## What Went Well

- Server-owned authority held across the epic. Surfaces and provider adapters do not self-assert sender authority.
- `draft-only` stayed a ChatBot-local record and did not become a Microsoft Graph draft or external send path.
- Outbound approval became a scoped, append-only record with proposed and approved content retained while audit/problem/log surfaces stay metadata-only.
- Send-time authority recomputation and evidence freshness enforcement moved from display concerns into execution gating.
- Inbound authenticity stayed non-blocking at intake but became available to association, review, audit, and projection flows.
- Strictness routing was implemented as a guard over deterministic scoring, not a hidden threshold rewrite.
- OpenAPI/client regeneration and generated-client fixture refreshes were performed as Epic 6 contract surfaces changed.

## Challenges

- Multiple material defects surfaced only during review:
  - Story 6.1 masked a missing outbound service-client grant as `approval-missing`.
  - Story 6.2 trusted submitted source actor identity too far.
  - Story 6.3 under-bound send execution to the paired approval request and later needed an `idempotency_conflict_outbound_send` catalog entry.
  - Story 6.4 initially folded only the first ordered `Authentication-Results` header.
  - Story 6.5 initially applied strictness routing to external-sender posture but not inbound authenticity anomalies, and later emitted a non-finite PascalCase routing reason.
- Metadata-only behavior had to be maintained across contracts, audit refs, problem details, worker results, projections, and generated clients.
- Story File Lists needed repeated reconciliation against actual changed files.
- The original Epic 6 retrospective identified a documentation audit need, but no Epic 6 documentation audit artifact existed before this rerun.

## Key Insights

1. Sender authority is a server decision, not a surface argument or provider permission mirror.
2. Approval records must bind the exact approved scope: command name, allowlist version, policy snapshot, source refs, sender authority, recipients, and context refs.
3. Evidence freshness is enforcement. Stale or expired send evidence cannot be a display-only warning.
4. Provider authenticity is evidence, not a ChatBot DNS verification responsibility.
5. External-sender posture and authenticity anomalies need one strictness path, or the tenant policy is misleading.
6. File Lists, OpenAPI/client hashes, and finite wire tokens are part of the safety surface in this repo.
