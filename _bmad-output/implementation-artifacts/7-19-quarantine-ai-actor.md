# Story 7.19: Quarantine AI actor

Status: done

<!-- Validation: create-story checklist applied 2026-06-02. -->

## Story

As an authorized **policy administrator**,
I want to quarantine a risky AI actor under a two-person rule,
so that risky AI activity is contained pending review — its **new proposals (and any other command) enter a review-only, contained state and fail closed at the command-admission pipeline** with safe next-action guidance, and cannot execute until quarantine is cleared — while every prior proposal and audit record stays intact and reconstructable.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Given an AI actor under investigation, when an authorized **human policy admin** proposes a **quarantine** and a **second** authorized human policy admin approves it (FR75d two-person rule), then the AI actor transitions to a durable **Quarantined** governance control state and **new proposals and commands from that AI actor enter a review-only/contained state and fail closed** at the command-admission pipeline (they cannot execute until quarantine is cleared), while **unrelated AI actors and other actors continue normally** (isolation, NFR30/NFR58). The quarantine is a security-sensitive FR74 governance control — **not** the Epic 5 Keycloak-sourced grant lifecycle (`ServiceClientGrant.IsRevoked`/`ExpiresAt`) — so it MUST go through the same submit→second-person-approve flow established for the Story 7.18 AI-actor disable (`SubmitAiActorDisable`→`ApproveAiActorDisable`) and the Story 7.16 service-client quarantine (`SubmitServiceClientQuarantine`→`ApproveServiceClientQuarantine`); a single-actor quarantine never takes effect. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.19`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR74`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75d`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-18-disable-ai-actor.md`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-16-quarantine-service-client.md`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Operations/GovernedOperationAggregate.cs:321`]

2. Given the quarantine proposal and approval, when authorization runs, then the operation is permitted only for a **human policy administrator** — gate on **`AdminAuthorityEvaluator.HasHumanAdminScope(actor.Principal, AdminScope.Policy)`** (AI-action governance is the policy-admin's domain per Story 7.2 "Policy-admin scope … and AI action policy"; the epic persona is explicitly "policy administrator"). This is the **same divergence Story 7.18 applied** and the **key difference from the 7.16 service-client quarantine**, which gated on `HasHumanTenantAdmin` because no finer service-client scope exists — here a finer scope (`AdminScope.Policy`) **does** exist and is the natural home. A `tenant-admin` still qualifies because `AdminScopes.ScopesForRole(TenantAdmin)` returns the full scope set (FR75a union) — so this is **not** a relaxation. The operation is **denied for service clients and AI actors** even when they carry admin-looking claims (the human-actor gate inside `HasHumanAdminScope`/`IsHumanActor`), and denied for non-policy human scopes (`mailbox-admin`/`compliance-admin`/`operations-admin`-only). The approving admin MUST be a **different person** from the proposer: both `RequesterRef != ApproverRef` (Ordinal safe-token compare) and `RequesterActorId != approver envelope UserId` are enforced — at the gateway validation stage, the accepted-command dispatcher, **and** re-checked in the aggregate, mirroring `IsValidAiActorDisableApproval`, `AcceptedCommandDispatcher`'s distinct-approver guard, and `GovernedOperationAggregate.Handle(ApproveAiActorDisable)`. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.19`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.2`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75a`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75d`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Governance/Admin/AdminAuthorityEvaluator.cs:10`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs:135`]

3. Given the quarantine is applied (approval committed), when it is recorded, then the operation records — with **no skip-audit path** (FR74, FR75g, NFR15a) — the **actor** (approver identity / actor type), **scope used** (`admin-scope:policy`), **subject** (the safe AI-actor ref / its `ServiceClientId`), **reason** (documented justification reason code), **old state** (`Active`), **new state** (`Quarantined`), **policy snapshot id**, and **timestamp**, via a metadata-only `AuditEnvelope` whose `StateTransition` is `"Active->Quarantined"`. The audit is written through the existing fail-closed pre-commit seam (`IAuditWriter.RecordPreCommitAsync` via `CommandGateway`): when the pre-commit audit is unavailable, **no durable quarantine state is written and no admission-blocking side effect occurs** (fail closed), reusing the disable/`CommandGateway` audit-unavailable behavior. No admin role bypasses NFR15a/NFR50a. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.19`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75g`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR15a`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Audit/AuditEnvelopeFactory.cs:1499`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/CommandGateway.cs`]

4. Given an AI actor is Quarantined, when that AI actor submits **any command** — including an `ExecuteLowRiskAIAssistance` / `ExecuteApprovedAIAction` AI proposal — then the authorization stage **fails closed before admission**: `ServiceClientGrantValidator` (which already runs for `service` **and** `ai` actor types) returns `ChatBotAuthorizationResult.Denied("ai_actor_quarantined")` for the `ai` actor whose control state is `Quarantined`, **before** the grant scope/allowlist checks and before the proposal ever reaches the downstream `AiActionApprovalGate` — so no AI proposal from the quarantined actor is admitted/executed and no restricted data is returned. The quarantine check sits **beside** the existing `ai_actor_disabled` check in the AI-actor-gated branch (after `IsRevoked`/`ExpiresAt`, before the service-client control-state block and before scope/allowlist). A proposal/command from any **other (still-Active)** AI actor for the same tenant is unaffected (isolation). The new `ai_actor_quarantined` reason is **distinct from** the 7.18 `ai_actor_disabled` (same two-person AI-actor governance family, different control state), from the 7.16 `service_client_quarantined` (the service-client subject class), and from the Epic 5 `service_client_grant_revoked` (Keycloak grant revocation). [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.19`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/ServiceClientGrantValidator.cs:88`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/ChatBotAuthorizationReasonCodes.cs:40`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Server/Gateway/Stages/AiActionApprovalGate.cs`]

5. Given an AI actor is quarantined, when its prior activity is inspected, then **all previously recorded proposals, commands, audit envelopes, and workflow items that originated from that AI actor remain visible, intact, and fully reconstructable** — quarantine affects **only future admission**, never already-committed records, proposals, associations, files, approvals, conversation content, or their audit trails (NFR17 visible/recoverable states; FR75c admins cannot mutate project-level records). Quarantine is reversible by a future release/re-activate flow; this story does not delete or rewrite any prior record. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR17`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR75c`; `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/7-18-disable-ai-actor.md`]

6. Given an actor encounters a quarantined AI actor (admin viewing status, or an admission routed to quarantine), when guidance is surfaced, then **safe recovery guidance** is shown from the **finite message catalog** — a safe headline (≤ 80 chars), a one-line reason from a finite set conveying "contained for review", a next-action pointing to the responsible role (review/release is a policy-admin/two-person action), and a `disabled-action` reason — using `ChatBotMessageCatalog` / `ChatBotMessageCodes` / `ChatBotDisabledActionReasons`, never raw error text. The guidance conveys that the AI actor is contained while evidence is reviewed and never names an unauthorized project/file/party/mailbox-content/audit detail (NFR2); EN/FR via existing localization; stable machine tokens (reason codes, state tokens, correlation ids) stay untranslated. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md#Story 7.19`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR76`; `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#NFR2`; `src/Hexalith.ChatBot.Contracts/Messages/ChatBotMessageCatalog.cs:470`]

7. Given the quarantine subject and audit refs are metadata, when the proposal/approval/quarantined events and the audit envelope are serialized, then they carry **only safe, finite tokens** — the safe AI-actor ref (its `ServiceClientId`), `admin-operation:ai-actor-quarantine[-approve]` / `admin-scope:policy`, the reason code, old/new state tokens (`active`/`quarantined`), policy-snapshot id, correlation id, UTC timestamp — and **never** service-client/AI credentials, OAuth grant fingerprints, bearer tokens, secrets, model prompts/completions, raw claims/headers, delegated-user PII, mailbox/project/proposal/evidence content, or raw GUIDs that are not already safe identifiers (NFR2, NFR4, NFR5). The subject is identified by its existing safe id (`ServiceClientGrant.ServiceClientId` via `AuditMetadata.SafeOptionalToken` / `IsSafeStableIdentifier`). Tenant scope comes **only** from the authenticated gateway binding — never from the command body. ## Dev Notes

### Scope Boundaries

- Story 7.19 is the **eighth** cell of the FR74 enforcement series (7.12–7.26: five subject classes — mailbox / service-client / **AI-actor** / command-capability / outbound — × three actions — disable / quarantine / rate-limit). It implements exactly **one** cell: **AI actor × quarantine**. Disable (7.18) and quarantine (this story) are **security-sensitive** admin operations gated by the **FR75d two-person rule**; rate-limit (7.20) is a standard policy mutation. **Do not** implement rate-limit, any other subject class, or a future quarantine-release/re-activate flow here. [Source: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Hexalith.ChatBot-2026-05-28/prd.md#FR74`]
- **This story is the 7.18 disable cell with `Quarantine`/`Quarantined` substituted for `Disable`/`Disabled` — i.e. it applies the 7.16 disable→quarantine substitution to the AI-actor row that 7.18 built.** Read 7.18 and 7.16 first. There are **two** things to carry forward together:
  1. **Authorization scope = policy-admin, not tenant-admin** (the 7.18 divergence). Gate on **`HasHumanAdminScope(actor.Principal, AdminScope.Policy)`** (as the AI-actor disable branches at `ParticipantAuthorizationStage.cs:135–147` do). 7.16 used `HasHumanTenantAdmin` because no finer service-client scope exists; for the AI actor a finer scope **does** exist (`AdminScope.Policy`, the AI-action policy domain per 7.2). A `tenant-admin` still passes via the FR75a scope union. **Rejected:** `HasHumanTenantAdmin` (too coarse; ignores the named policy-admin persona) and a new `AdminScope.AiActor` (scope-creep).
  2. **Quarantine ≈ Disable at the AI-actor admission seam — the one meaningful semantic difference** (the 7.16 lesson). For an AI actor, "new proposals enter a review-only state and cannot execute" = new commands/proposals **fail closed before admission** at `ServiceClientGrantValidator`, with a **distinct reason code, state token, lifecycle transition, and safe next-action guidance** (`quarantined` / "contained for review" vs `disabled` / "blocked until re-enabled"). There is **no** intake-routing branch and **no** worker change — admission for an AI actor is enforced entirely in the Authorization stage (the same as the 7.16 service-client quarantine, which is the key structural difference from the 7.13 mailbox quarantine that did touch an intake worker).
- **Same as 7.18 (preserve the two-person machinery):** submit→second-person-approve; a single-actor quarantine only ever produces a pending approval. Distinct approver enforced **three times** (gateway validator, accepted-command dispatcher, aggregate re-check), `RequesterRef != ApproverRef` Ordinal AND `RequesterActorId != envelope.UserId`. Control state via the fixed FR74 path, never the Epic 5 grant-lifecycle (`IsRevoked`). Terminal catalog guidance (`request-access` + `disabled-action`), not transient.
- Quarantine affects **future admission only** (including future AI proposals). It must never delete, rewrite, or hide already-committed proposals, commands, audit envelopes, associations, files, approvals, or conversation content (NFR17, FR75c). It is reversible by a future release flow (out of scope here).
- Tenant identity is always from the **authenticated gateway binding**, never the command body. The subject (AI actor) and all audit refs are **metadata-only, safe tokens** (NFR2); credentials, OAuth grant fingerprints, model prompts/completions, and delegated-user PII never appear (NFR4/NFR5).

### "AI proposals" — what gets blocked and where (unchanged from 7.18)

- AI proposals are submitted as **commands** by the AI actor: `ExecuteLowRiskAIAssistance` (and `ExecuteApprovedAIAction`). They run through the shared command pipeline (FR81a): **Authorization stage** (`ParticipantAuthorizationStage` → `ServiceClientGrantValidator`) → **Approval gate** (`AiActionApprovalGate` → `DefaultAiActionPolicyEvaluator`). Authorization runs first, so blocking a quarantined AI actor at `ServiceClientGrantValidator` is **fail-closed before** the proposal reaches `AiActionApprovalGate` or any policy evaluation. **No** change to `AiActionApprovalGate`/`DefaultAiActionPolicyEvaluator` is needed (and none should be made) — the quarantined-actor denial is upstream of policy evaluation, exactly as the disabled-actor denial is.
